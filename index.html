    SaaS Development

    What Is SaaS — And What Could It Do For You?

    Software as a Service is simply software you log into rather than software you install. That one change has knock-on effects worth understanding — whether you want to use it, or build one of your own.

    What it actually means

    Instead of buying software and installing it on a machine in your office, you open it in a web browser and pay a monthly or annual fee for as long as you use it. The company that built it takes care of the hosting, the security, the backups and the updates. You log in and work.

    You are almost certainly using several already — Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Xero and Sage all work this way.

    What it replaces

    The older model: buy a licence up front, install it on a server in the office, buy and maintain that server, pay someone to keep it patched and backed up — then pay again in a few years when your version stops being supported.

    If the office loses power or that one machine fails, so does the software — and often so does the only copy of the data.

    Two ways this applies to your business

    People use the word "SaaS" to mean two quite different things. Both are worth knowing before you decide what to build.

    Path 1 · Most common

    Software for your own business, delivered as a service

    This is what most of our work looks like. Your application lives online and your team simply logs in.

    • Nothing to install, and no server to buy or maintain
    • Works on a laptop at the office, a phone on site, a tablet in the field
    • Backups, security patches and updates handled for you
    • A predictable running cost instead of a large once-off outlay
    • Add or remove users as your team changes
    • New staff have access on their first day
    • One version of the system, so everyone really is looking at the same data
    Path 2 · A product of your own

    A SaaS product you own and sell to others

    If the problem you have is a problem your whole industry has, the system you build to solve it can become something you sell.

    • Recurring subscription income rather than once-off project revenue
    • Build it once, sell it many times over
    • Often starts life as an internal tool that turned out to be worth more outside
    • Becomes a business asset in its own right, not just an operating expense
    • Direct insight into how customers actually use what you have built
    • You control the roadmap — it grows in the direction your market pulls it
